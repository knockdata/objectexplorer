# Heroes

The people, companies and groups you follow, with what they published lately in one list, newest
first: blog posts, videos, GitHub pushes and stars, comments. A part of the [Browser](/explore/browser),
so on macOS and Windows.

In the Browser's side bar, **Heroes** shows them. **+** in the header, or **Browser: Add a Hero** in
the command palette, adds one: a name, and their links, one a line — a blog, a YouTube channel, a
GitHub, Mastodon, Bluesky, Hacker News or Reddit profile, or any page. **Search the Web for This
Name** looks the name up with DuckDuckGo in a tab, and offers the full name it finds.

## What a hero shows

Each link is followed where it publishes for free: a blog's own feed (or its sitemap, or the page
listing its writing), YouTube, Mastodon, Bluesky, GitHub's public activity, Hacker News, Reddit and
Medium. Each item says what it is — an article, a video, a post, a push, a star, a release, a pull
request — and a click opens it in the tab you are on.

The hero with the newest item comes first, with five items and **Load More…**. A click on a hero's
icon hides their items, and again shows them. Beside the name are **Edit Hero**, **Refresh This Hero**
and **Remove Hero**.

On a Mac, an X or LinkedIn link shows the first posts of its page, read with your sign-in to that
site in the Browser; a page that asks you to sign in says so, and opens the site's sign-in in a tab.
Elsewhere, and for any other site with no free feed, the link stays a link that opens in a tab.

## When it asks

Links are asked when the Heroes show and when you press refresh in the header (**Browser: Refresh
Heroes** in the palette). A link asked less than ten minutes ago is not asked again until you
refresh, and a feed that has not changed answers with nothing. Nothing paid is ever called. What was
found is kept on this machine.
